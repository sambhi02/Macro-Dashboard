**November 2026** · Bottom line: The Fed's Inflation fight isn't over, and higher-for-longer rates are the base case. 

- **Policy:** The Fed raised rates in September to a 3.75-4% range and held in October, but two members wanted another hike. With the December 8-9 meeting ahead, a further increase is a real possibility, while a cut is unlikely. 
- **Jobs & Rates:** Employers added 165,000 jobs in October, well above the past years's pace, and unemployment sits around 4.1%. A solid job market gives Fed room to stay tight. The 10-year Treasury is near 4.9%, and the yield curve is only slightly positive (+0.24 pts). 
- **Inflation & jobs:** Prices rose 0..4% in October, more than expected, and core CPI Inflation ran at 0.3% a month. The last annual reading was 3.7% (September), still well above the Fed's 2% target. Inflation is sticky, not falling. 
- **What it means for clients:** Cash and short-term Treasuries still pay well, so excess savings shouldn't it in 0% checking. With 10-year yields near 5%, it's a good time to lock in some longer-term bond income, though expect bond price swings while inflation stays hot. Borrowers shouldn't plan around near-term rate cuts.
